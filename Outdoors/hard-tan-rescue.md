How to soften a stiff tanned hide without manual breaking.

### Supplies
- TruBond Lubie Stretch 2000


### 1. Thin Down the Leather (Sanding)
- **Bulk Removal:** Use an angle grinder equipped with an 80-grit flap disc on the thickest sections of the hide (e.g., shoulders and tail).
- **Detail Sanding:** Switch to a palm/orbital sander with 40-grit sandpaper for lighter areas (like the belly) and to smooth out the transition lines.
- **Safety Check:** Constantly feel the leather thickness with your hand. Stop sanding immediately if hair follicles begin to show to keep from ruining the hide or weakening the stitching strength.


### 2. Re-Wet & Sweat the Hide
- **Prepare Solution:** Mix 10 oz of cold water with ~2 tablespoons of fine-grain, non-iodized white salt.
- **Application:** Spray the solution evenly on the skin side until it turns from white to a fleshy/yellowish wet tone.
- **Sweating Phase:** Fold the hide skin-to-skin, wrap or bag it, and let it absorb the moisture in a cool/shady spot (or refrigerator) for a minimum of 2 hours.


### 3. Apply Tanning Oil / Lube
- **Prepare Oil Mix:** Combine a 50/50 mixture of hot water with *TruBond Lubie Stretch 2000*.
- **Application:** Spread a thin, even coat across the entire skin side, ensuring full coverage out to the edges without pooling.
- **Sweating Phase:** Fold the hide skin-to-skin again, bag it, and allow it to sweat for at least 12 hours (or overnight in a fridge) so the oil penetrates the entire fiber matrix.


### 4. Slow Air-Drying & Periodic Stretching
- **Air Dry:** Lay the hide out flat in a cool area away from direct sunlight or heat sources.
- **Periodic Pulling:** Every 12 to 24 hours while drying, gently pull and stretch the hide in all directions for a couple of minutes to open the leather fibers and work the oil in.
- **Completion:** Once fully dry, the hide will be soft and supple.

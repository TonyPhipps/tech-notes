# Food Sources
- **Transition zones** offer ideal, mixed food sources close to safety escapes. Trace where dark forest meets lighter open slope.
- **Meadows** appear as bright green or tan patches amid timber, often flat benches or drainages.
- **Recent burns** have plenty of young growth vegetation. Toggle the "Historic Wildfires" layer — target areas 1–5 years post-burn.

# Bedding Areas / Safety Escapes
- **North- and east-facing slopes** are cooler, shaded, less wind — ideal for daytime bedding.
- **Mature timber / thick cover** appear as dense, uniform dark-green canopy on satellite.
- **Benches** give game security + a view + quick escape routes. Look for subtle flattening in contour lines (a little plateau before a drop-off).
- **Finger ridges** that branch from main ridgelines. Deer bed along sides just below the crest.
- **Thermal & wind advantage** are well tracked by the game. Midday, air rises up-slope; evening, it falls down-slope. game understand where they can best smell predators.
- **Saddles** offer a good bridge between areas and quick scouting opportunity for game to feel safe.

# Water Sources
- **Creeks** are indicated by solid blue lines and can be perennial or seasonal.
- **Drainages** may appear as small blue dashes or where vegetation is lush green compared to surroundings. Look for v-shaped draws with greener satellite coloration.
- **Seeps** often appear in burned or open country as tiny clusters of bright green shrubs.
- **Troughs / tanks / stock ponds** installed by Ranch or Forest Service can hold water late into the season.
- **Small man-made ponds** appear as circular or rectangular dark spots near trails or two-tracks.

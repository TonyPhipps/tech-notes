# Food Sources
- Transition zones. Deer like edges between thick timber (cover) and open feed (visibility trade-off). Trace where dark forest meets lighter open slope. Look for finger ridges and benches that have mixed vegetation.
- Meadows. bright green or tan patches amid timber, often flat benches or drainages.
- Recent burns. Toggle the "Historic Wildfires" layer — target areas 1–5 years post-burn.

# Bedding Areas / Safety Escapes
- North- and east-facing slopes. Cooler, shaded, less wind — ideal for daytime bedding.
- Dense, uniform dark-green canopy on satellite = mature timber / thick cover.
- Benches give deer security + a view + quick escape routes. On topo, look for subtle flattening in contour lines (a little plateau before a drop-off).
- Trace finger ridges that branch from main ridgelines. Deer bed along sides just below the crest.
- Track thermal & wind advantage. Midday, air rises up-slope; evening, it falls down-slope. Bucks bed where they can smell both up and down the hill.
- Slopes where prevailing winds cross thermals - e.g., north-facing benches near ridges or just below saddles.

# Water Sources
- **Creeks** are indicated by solid blue lines and can be perennial or seasonal.
- **Drainages** may appear as small blue dashes or where vegetation is lush green compared to surroundings. Look for v-shaped draws with greener satellite coloration.
- **Seeps** often appear in burned or open country as tiny clusters of bright green shrubs.
- **Troughs / tanks / stock ponds** installed by Ranch or Forest Service can hold water late into the season.
- **Small man-made ponds** appear as circular or rectangular dark spots near trails or two-tracks.
